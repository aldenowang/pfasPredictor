ForeverCatch

Predicting forever chemical (PFAS) contamination in fish

Pick where a fish was caught, choose the species and enter its length. The model estimates how much PFAS the fish may contain, based on how close the spot is to military bases and urban areas, its elevation, and how large the fish is for its species. For fish caught in the United States only.

How to read these numbers

  • Exact prediction is our single best estimate, from an Extra Trees machine-learning model with a median error of 3.04 ppb. Individual fish can still be much higher or lower.
  • Upper bounds are the levels we are 95% and 99% confident the PFAS in this fish falls below.

Deciding whether to eat it? Use the 95% upper bound, not the exact prediction, which can be far off. The bound is cautious on purpose, especially near sea level, where it can reach about 400 ppb. Compare it with the chart below and check your state's fish advisories.

How often can you eat it?

This chart is from the Great Lakes Consortium for Fish Consumption Advisories (2025). It shows how often it's OK to eat a fish based on how much PFOS is in its meat.

PFOS is one type of PFAS, and it's the main one found in fish. In a study of freshwater fish across the U.S., PFOS made up about 74% of the total PFAS on average.

PFOS in the fish (ppb)How often you can eat it
0 to 2.54 or more meals a week
Over 2.5 to 52 meals a week
Over 5 to 101 meal a week
Over 10 to 401 meal a month
Over 40Do not eat

Our estimates above count all PFAS together, not just PFOS, so the PFOS in your fish is usually a bit lower than our number. For a cautious read, compare the chart with the 95% upper bound above, not the exact prediction. Sources: Best Practice for PFOS Guidelines, Table 1 (September 2025); Barbo et al., Environmental Research (2023).
