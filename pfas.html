The Chemicals That Never LeaveWhy PFAS in freshwater fish matters

PFAS, often called "forever chemicals," break down very slowly and build up in fish and in the people who eat them. A 2023 analysis of more than 500 EPA composite fish samples found that freshwater fish across the U.S. carry far more PFAS than fish sold in stores.

The EPA links PFAS exposure to some cancers (kidney, testicular and prostate), lower fertility, developmental effects in children, a weaker immune response and higher cholesterol.

  • 278x higher median PFAS in U.S. freshwater fish than in store-bought fish.
  • One serving a year of a typical freshwater fish can noticeably raise PFOS levels in the blood.
  • Zero is the EPA's health-based goal for PFOA and PFOS in drinking water.
Sources: Barbo et al., Environmental Research (2023); U.S. EPA, health effects of PFAS; U.S. EPA, PFAS drinking water standard.

How official advice uses PFOS

Many fish advisories are based on PFOS, a single PFAS compound measured in a lab. For example, the Great Lakes Consortium for Fish Consumption Advisories recommends these meal limits by PFOS level in the fish:

PFOS in the fish (ppb)Suggested meals
0 to 2.54 or more meals a week
Over 2.5 to 52 meals a week
Over 5 to 101 meal a week
Over 10 to 401 meal a month
Over 40Do not eat

These limits apply to lab-measured PFOS. ForeverCatch estimates total PFAS with a model, so its numbers can’t be read against this chart. For advice about a specific water body, check your state’s fish advisory program. Source: Great Lakes Consortium, Best Practice for PFOS Guidelines, Table 1 (September 2025).
