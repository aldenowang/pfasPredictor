Why I Built This

I’ve been fishing since I was three, and growing up, I loved bringing home my catches to cook and eat with my family.

But in my freshman year of high school, my family stopped eating the fish I caught.

We’d become concerned about “forever chemicals” (PFAS) contaminating freshwater fish across the country. A 2023 study found that eating just one freshwater fish could expose someone to as much PFOS as a month of drinking certain contaminated water.

It frustrated me that I could spend hours trying to catch a fish, yet have no idea whether it was safe to eat.

Laboratory testing costs hundreds of dollars and can take weeks, making it impractical for most recreational anglers. And because PFAS is measured in parts per billion, there’s no way to reliably detect it just by looking at a fish.

So I spent the next two years developing ForeverCatch, using publicly available environmental data to estimate PFAS contamination in freshwater fish.

While it can’t replace laboratory testing or official fish-consumption advisories, I hope ForeverCatch helps other anglers better understand the potential contamination in their catches.
